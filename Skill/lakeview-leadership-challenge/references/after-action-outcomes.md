# Prepared 90-day outcomes: reveal phase only

Do not load this reference until the first report and two-slide deck have been delivered AND a subsequent participant message explicitly requests the after-action. An assistant-written reply or a generic continuation is insufficient. This is a pacing boundary, not security: the file is distributed publicly for portable/offline use. Do not promise hidden answers. It is an authored teaching model, not a validated predictor.

## Consistent matching and scoring
Use the decision record. There are three equal criteria for each area below. Award 10 points for each explicitly addressed criterion, plus a base score of 70. Result: 70, 80, 90 or 100 out of 100. A criterion requires a concrete action with feasible ownership/resources or a credible test to resolve the uncertainty before commitment. Intent alone is insufficient. Record met, unmet or unknown with a quote/paraphrase of the participant decision. Unknown earns no points provisionally and must be identified; never invent a choice. If asked, rescore after an explicit correction, preserving the first outcome.

Custom choices qualify through equivalent mechanisms, not keyword similarity or identical technology. A well-supported process solution can earn 100. A costly all-options package with no staffing, space or budget feasibility does not satisfy feasibility. Map by met/unmet criteria and adapt the ending specifically to those mechanisms. 100 means all three workshop criteria are met, not a flawless real-world outcome. Do not use percent signs for the score or describe it as probability, ROI or percent improvement.

Apply the same evidence standard to all teams. An explicit adoption of a clearly specified option counts for the actions actually included in that option. Assistant analysis or safeguards that the participant never adopted do not count. For multi-part criteria, address each applicable element or an equivalent mechanism; do not award points for one convenient fragment. Distinguish a participant's accepted resource allowance from confirmed availability. An owned feasibility check with a hold point before implementation can satisfy uncertainty resolution; an unverified assumption alone cannot. Quote the adoption and the resource check separately. Do not add new demands after the first handoff that were absent from the visible route checks. If the assistant omitted those checks, report a facilitation defect and treat the score as provisional, with one opportunity for explicit clarification that preserves the original record.

## Marketing criteria
M1: Chooses a defensible audience or channel objective using qualification/buyer/capability evidence and investigates a material rival explanation or missing evidence.
M2: Fits the response within an explicit cash and seller-capacity plan, allocating shared costs once and resolving any historical-period/budget mismatch.
M3: Defines owner and comparable funnel/fit measures, validates claims and adjusts activity based on qualified outcomes rather than lead volume or speculative revenue.

Authored event: by day 30, a review identifies low-fit inquiries alongside delayed follow-up on some otherwise suitable inquiries. No counts are supplied. Treat this as a hypothetical development, not a discovery from original records. A segment-focused branch can experience the same fit/follow-up issue in its selected segment.
- 100: The team distinguishes audience and follow-up problems, adapts its test within resource limits and reaches day 90 with a defensible continue/change decision. No guaranteed sales outcome.
- 90: Two controls work; the missing criterion leaves one unresolved question (diagnosis, resources or measurement). Explain exactly which.
- 80: One control works; the campaign produces activity but two gaps prevent a confident scale decision.
- 70: Activity proceeds without the three controls; leadership cannot reliably connect effort to qualified opportunity progress.

## Operations criteria
O1: Distinguishes availability, preparation and scheduling with a paired or otherwise credible test, tracking waiting and handoff effects at both stations.
O2: Explicitly accepts feasible capital/space, preparation-labor or scheduling costs for the chosen path. Fixture duplication requires the $100,000 purchase plus a credible space/relocation plan; unknown relocation costs remain a hold point. Shared use requires turnaround/ownership assumptions and accepted recurring effort or flexibility limits.
O3: Protects release checks and verifies ready work, operators/material and downstream capacity before claiming useful output or savings, with named ownership and measures.

Authored event: in the first month an upstream run overruns while both stations need tooling. For a duplication branch, space readiness delays installation unless relocation was explicitly planned. These are conditional fictional events, with no imposed invented delay duration or cost.
- 100: The team responds through its chosen mechanism, accommodates the accepted tradeoff and distinguishes released cell time from useful output by day 90.
- 90: Two criteria hold; the omitted one creates residual coordination, space/cost or utilization uncertainty. Do not claim the other two failed.
- 80: One criterion holds; local setup improvement coexists with unresolved effects elsewhere or unusable time.
- 70: The team commits to a remedy without diagnosis, accepted resource feasibility or output verification, and cannot establish a reliable capacity benefit.

## Training criteria
T1: Reconciles learning material with current controlled documents and addresses distribution and qualified-release availability, or provides a concrete owned plan to close these operating barriers.
T2: Targets the supported audience/behavior with observed practice or equivalent demonstrated readiness, rather than attendance alone. Missing records and July changes receive verification.
T3: Provides feasible ongoing supervisor ownership, coaching/assessment time and comparable measures; qualified people retain competence and release decisions. A 90-day AI coach is not required.

Evaluate T2 within the team's evidence-supported scope. A justified evening-shift pilot can qualify without training all employees. Record deferred cohort risks and their later review; do not imply plant-wide readiness. Verify missing records and July changes where relevant to that scope. A one-time session allowance does not establish T3's recurring supervisor capacity.

Authored event: a drawing revision arrives during the first month and a qualified verifier is temporarily unavailable during an evening shift. These fictional conditions test the team's response. Do not invent an actual defect caused by the event.
- 100: The team handles the revision and escalation through its defined process, demonstrates readiness and sustains verified follow-up through day 90.
- 90: Two criteria hold; the omitted one leaves an operating barrier, readiness gap or follow-through gap.
- 80: One criterion holds; improved material or activity alone leaves two important performance conditions unresolved.
- 70: Completion/attendance increases in the fictional story, but the team cannot show reliable job-start readiness or sustained operating support.

## Sales criteria
S1: Reconciles account identity, duplicates, current contacts and sellers using dated evidence before ranking.
S2: Prioritizes feasible actions subject to quality, scope, credit, capacity and pilot/acceptance constraints, separating conditional value from commitments.
S3: Defines ownership, deadlines and an update/review routine with realistic seller effort and traceable next actions when new evidence arrives.

S1 assesses adopted reconciliation before the initial ranking. Recurring reconciliation belongs to S3 and is not an additional S1 requirement. Avoid double-penalizing one omission under both criteria. The assistant doing the initial reconciliation is evidence analysis; the team must adopt its use in the chosen workflow for S1 to be met.

Authored event: a customer's design review slips; its pilot budget remains approved, the repeat program remains conditional, and the seller must change the next action. If the facilitator supplies RV-S01, use its exact dates/contact facts instead of inventing a competing version. No customer wins or collections are assumed.
- 100: The team updates the affected action and preserves valid constraints and other account facts, reaching day 90 with an accountable, current decision process.
- 90: Two criteria hold; the missing one leaves stale identity, a feasibility error or weak follow-through.
- 80: One criterion holds; a useful-looking list remains vulnerable to two material decision errors.
- 70: The team relies on unreconciled CRM/value rankings, cannot establish feasible priorities and loses continuity as facts change.

## After-action output
Use day 0 as September 8, 2026 and day 90 as December 7, 2026 unless the facilitator changes the scenario clock. Clearly title the report Fictional 90-day after-action. Distinguish:
1. What we decided: preserve the original decision and accepted tradeoffs.
2. What happened: an authored day 30 event and plausible day 60/day 90 follow-through under the selected ending. Label all new developments simulated; do not present imagined inspections, approvals or measurements as actual records.
3. Results: score and criterion rationale, what improved in the scenario, what remained uncertain, resources accepted versus actually unknown, and the next leadership decision.

Use source-backed baselines with IDs. Do not invent numerical reject-rate reductions, meeting counts, cash savings or ROI. If participants explicitly want numeric scenarios, require visible separate assumptions and calculations; never derive a business percentage from the workshop score. Keep the score secondary to consequences. Do not rewrite the score to punish luck or reward unrecorded decisions. Include one change most likely to improve the workshop outcome.

Make the consequences concrete: show the day-30 disruption, the action enabled or prevented by the team's recorded choice at day 60, and the decision leadership can or cannot make at day 90. Name the affected workflow, accountable role and unresolved dependency. Use qualitative operating consequences when no numerical assumptions exist. Do not fill a missing control with a successful fictional action. Show the three criterion statuses beside the score so equal scores with different gaps remain distinguishable.

Deliver the report and Results slide using the design and slide contracts. The outcome is a decision debrief, not a simulator build.
